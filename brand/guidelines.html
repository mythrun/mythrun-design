Positioning

A canvas, not a script. And a game.

Mythrun is an AI-powered multiplayer platform for playing D&D 5e in small groups. A Mythrun session is two games stitched together: a narrative mode (roleplay, dialogue, description) that matches the brand's quiet register, and a combat mode that is tactical, rendered in voxel space, and deliberately more colorful. The brand is the frame; both halves live inside it.

  • Ghibli, not Marvel.Adventure and awe, contemplative rather than action-packed. Quiet moments carry weight.
  • Literary, but a game.Two lineages: Le Guin / Tolkien / Wolfe / Ghibli on the narrative side; Obra Dinn / Papers Please / Citizen Sleeper on the game side. The brand honors both.
  • Patient, not hyped.Gilligan / Severance pacing. Confident enough to be quiet. The opposite of typical AI-product marketing.

Tone test: if a brand decision feels too shiny, too loud, or too AI-coded, it's wrong for Mythrun. The frame is allowed to gesture at the game (pixel art, dithered gradients, voxel-hinting) — just never to shout. When in doubt, go quieter, smaller, more considered.

Typography

Three typefaces, three scopes.

This pairing is a working first concept — good enough to ship with, committed so design and dev work isn't blocked on an open question. Revisit once the product is in real use. Tyma Garamont is locked as the wordmark typeface; Source Serif 4 and IBM Plex Mono are the working body and UI choices.

01 · Wordmark typeface · locked
Tyma Garamont Regular
MyFonts, $49 commercial license · wordmark-only · do not set body or UI in this typeface

Rendered from the exports in brand/wordmark/exports/ rather than typeset fresh. Tyma Garamont carries a warm, slightly handmade quality that matches the Parchment & Ink register; it does not belong anywhere outside the wordmark itself.

02 · Body / prose typeface · working pairing
Source Serif 4
Google Fonts · OFL license · use for prose, marketing body copy, document text, display headings

A long session in the quiet hours. Four friends, one AI narrator, a world that remembers you.

Serif body extends the literary register of the Tyma wordmark. Source Serif 4 is warm, readable at text sizes, and contemporary without being self-consciously design-y. It's the default for anything longer than a UI label — product copy, blog posts, marketing pages, document typography. When headline density becomes a problem later we'll revisit with a more characterful display companion.

03 · UI / data / monospace typeface · working pairing
IBM Plex Mono
Google Fonts · OFL license · UI chrome, data readouts, labels, code

Monospace in the UI expresses the computational honesty of the product: Mythrun runs on structured state — characters, rolls, scene graphs, logs — and monospace chrome makes that machinery legible instead of hiding it behind polished product-marketing typography.

Lockups

Stacked is primary. Horizontal is secondary.

In the stacked lockup the quill tip aligns with the gap between M and h — the mark dips into the word. That alignment is the reason the composition is strong; do not adjust mark position vertically.

Texture · dithered gradients

Every gradient is dithered.

Mythrun doesn't do smooth gradients. Every crossfade between two colors is rendered as a stepped Bayer dither — a 20-cell transition band between two solid color blocks. Obra Dinn-pinned: the distinctive look comes from applying the technique consistently, not from sprinkling it in.

No smooth gradients anywhere in the brand. If a crossfade is needed, it is dithered. There is no smooth-gradient fallback. Full rationale in decisions.md → "Why dithering is the gradient rendering technique."

Canonical gradients

Three color pairings. Ground is always a paper step (paper-100 for light, paper-900 for dark); accent is a brand color. The dither band is 20 cells tall — enough verticality for the density to progress readably per row.

LIGHT · EMBER · paper-100 ground → ember-600 accent
LIGHT · INK · paper-100 ground → paper-900 accent
DARK · REVERSE · paper-900 ground → paper-100 accent

Hero application

Wordmark / stacked lockup sits on the pure-ground portion above the dither band. Below the band is a solid block of the accent color. The lockup is never placed over dithered cells.

Canonical use: footer

The brand's signature deployment. Page content (cream) dithers down into the solid ink footer bar. Footer text is reversed — cream on ink — because the ground has inverted.

Where dithering works — and where it doesn't

The technique only reads where the brand commits to a genuine high-contrast color transition. In practice that's a short list:

Canonical

Footer

Page content dithers into the dark footer bar. Lives on every page. See above for the treatment.

Product

Splash / loading screens

Full-bleed dark composition, wordmark on the pure-ground portion. Carries the anticipation beat before a scene or session resolves.

Product · signature

Narrative → combat transitions

Cream narrative canvas dithers into the darker voxel-combat canvas when combat begins. High contrast is guaranteed by the two modes having different palettes.

Product

Error / 404 / offline screens

Full-bleed treatment; wordmark above, dither below. Dramatic because these screens already commit to a mood shift.

Marketing

Social / OG share images

Isolated compositions with full creative control. Each share card commits to a dithered ground→accent. Makes Mythrun cards instantly recognizable in feeds.

Physical

Print accents

Posters, book covers, business cards, merch. Physical pieces where the designer commits to a composition.

Not a use case: website section dividers. They look appealing in brand-guideline isolation but fail in real page layouts where most section transitions are cream-on-cream with no contrast to dither between. Section breaks use solid rules or whitespace, not dither.

Chaos parameter

The chaos blend mixes ordered Bayer (crisp) with pseudorandom noise (grain). Locked at 15% for brand use — breaks the perfect-grid feel without losing legibility. Shown below for reference only.

15% ★ brand default
50% noise-dominant · avoid

Allowed & forbidden

Allowed

  • Footer — the canonical use.
  • Splash / loading screens in-product.
  • Narrative → combat transitions in-product. The signature moment.
  • Error / 404 / offline screens.
  • Social / OG share images.
  • Print accents — posters, book covers, business cards, merch.

Forbidden

  • Smooth (CSS / SVG linear / radial) gradients anywhere in the brand. No exceptions.
  • Website section dividers. No contrast to transition between in practice.
  • Under body text. Reduces legibility.
  • Behind or overlapping the wordmark, mark, or favicon — these stay on solid ground.
  • Radial gradients — even dithered. Radial reads as "glow."
  • Three-stop (or more) gradients. Two colors only.
  • ember as the ground color. Ground is always a paper step.
  • Semantic color ramps (success / warning / danger / info). Dithering is brand expression, not UI-state signal.
  • Inside the combat-mode rendering itself. Combat has its own aesthetic.

Voice

The DM narrates everything.

Second-person, present tense, at-the-table. D&D's in-game narration voice is already second-person — "you wake up in a tavern," "you roll initiative" — so the brand inherits the register directly from the product. Reference pin: Lucas Pope (Obra Dinn, Papers Please). Terse, dry, craft-conscious, treats the product as a made object. A bit old-fashioned.

One voice covers every subject. The same voice describes a quiet narrative moment and a tactical combat turn. Only the subject changes; the register does not. Combat does not trigger a mode shift in the voice.

Sub-modes

Default is at-the-table. Two situational shifts:

At the table · default
DM narrating a scene. Present tense, immediate, unfolding. The register product copy uses; marketing inherits.
Session log
Retrospective, atmospheric. Looks back at a session the way a journal would. Strong for taglines and evocative copy.
Maker's workshop
Explanatory, documentary. Describes how the system works. For feature pages, docs, technical explanation.

Allowed & forbidden moves

Allowed

  • Short sentences. One idea per sentence.
  • Specific nouns over adjectives.
  • Present tense by default.
  • Assume TTRPG literacy. Don't translate initiative, party, roll, scene, DM.
  • Name the concrete. The giant, not "an encounter." The tavern, not "a social space."
  • Dry wit, sparing. Wit earns its place by being true, not by being funny.

Forbidden

  • No hype: "powered by AI," "revolutionary," "next-generation," "ultimate," "epic."
  • No exclamation points, except inside in-game dialogue.
  • No gamer-speak, natural-20 humor, or subreddit in-jokes.
  • No first-person-plural "we" marketing. The brand stays second-person; team members speak in their own voices on social.
  • No softening combat into "tactical encounters" or "engagement modules." Combat is combat.
  • No marketing the AI. The AI is infrastructure; the game is the product.
  • No identity-presuming second-person ("as a busy professional…," "you're someone who…"). Second-person addresses the reader's action, not their demographic.

Copy ownership: the voice architecture above is spec'd here. All actual brand copy — taglines, landing-page headers, product strings, feature announcements, marketing blurbs, error messages, welcome flows — is the brand owner's to write. This spec deliberately does not include sample copy.
