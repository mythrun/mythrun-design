# Mythrun Brand Brief v0.4

## What Mythrun is

Mythrun is an AI-powered multiplayer platform for playing D&D 5e in small groups (2-5 players). Three-agent Claude architecture: Fast Thinker handles player-facing DM work, Slow Thinker handles meta-narrative direction, Scribe maintains world state. Session-pack billing, one host pays.

## Positioning

Mythrun is for people who want to **play** D&D — not DM it, not recruit for it, not schedule six adults for a weekly game that lasts three months. The AI handles the logistics, the world-building scaffolding, the rule lookups. Players get to be players.

The product is **a canvas, not a script.** Mythrun doesn't run pre-authored adventures on rails. The AI generates a responsive world that the players shape through their choices. The best sessions will feel like collaboratively-authored fiction, not like reading-comprehension of someone else's story.

## Two modes

A Mythrun session is two games stitched together. The **narrative mode** — roleplay, dialogue, description, decisions — is the register the brand most obviously speaks to. The **combat mode** is tactical, rendered in voxel space, and fills most of the screen when active. Combat has its own aesthetic (retro-voxel, muted-but-contrasty, deliberately more colorful than the brand frame); the brand frame itself stays muted, but it holds the combat content without embarrassment. The brand is **not** only the narrative half. Mythrun is a game — it just happens to be one that reads.

## Register

**Ghibli, not Marvel.** Adventure and awe, contemplative rather than action-packed. Quiet moments matter as much as combat encounters. The tone is curious and warm, not epic and self-serious. Room for humor, smallness, ordinary details.

**Literary, but a game.** Two lineages run through the brand. On the narrative side: Le Guin, Tolkien, Gene Wolfe, Ghibli — literary fantasy and contemplative storytelling. On the game side: Obra Dinn, Papers Please, Citizen Sleeper — craft-games whose restraint is itself the marketing. Mythrun belongs to both lineages. The brand honors the tabletop tradition without pretending combat isn't there and without softening tactical play into "encounters." The quiet frame holds both halves.

**Patient, not hyped.** Vince Gilligan / Severance pacing — willing to sit with a moment, not constantly escalating. This is the opposite of most AI product marketing, which is all sparkles and excitement. Mythrun is confident enough to be quiet.

## What Mythrun is not

- Not AI-slop. The AI is infrastructure, not the value proposition. Don't lead with "powered by AI."
- Not a D&D replacement. Players who love human-DM groups should keep playing in them. Mythrun serves the enormous group of people who want to play D&D but can't make the logistics work.
- Not for competitive or min-max play. Mythrun optimizes for collaborative storytelling, not for rules-lawyering or optimized builds.
- Not for everyone. Early users will be curious, patient, and willing to forgive rough edges. Scale later; nail the tone first.

## Target communities

Avoid: r/DnD, r/dndnext (mainstream D&D communities are hostile to AI products).

Target: r/solorpgaming, LitRPG Adventures orbit, EN World's AI subforum, indie TTRPG podcast listeners, Obsidian/Roam note-takers who care about worldbuilding tools.

## Voice

**Default positioning: at-the-table.** The brand voice sounds like a DM narrating a scene — second-person, present tense, unfolding and immediate. This isn't a marketing convention. D&D's in-game voice is already second-person ("you wake up in a tavern," "you roll initiative"), so the brand inherits that register directly from the product. When Mythrun speaks in marketing copy, it speaks the way it speaks at the table.

**Reference pin:** Lucas Pope (*Obra Dinn*, *Papers Please*). Terse, dry, craft-conscious, treats the product as a made object. A bit old-fashioned. Humor is dry wit, used sparingly — never softens, hypes, or winks.

**One voice, every subject.** The same voice describes a quiet prose moment and a tactical combat turn. Only the subject changes, not the register. Combat does not trigger a mode shift in the voice.

**Sub-modes (situational, not defaults):**
- *Session log* — retrospective, atmospheric. Good for taglines and evocative copy.
- *Maker's workshop* — explanatory, documentary. Good for feature pages and docs.

### Allowed moves

- Short sentences. One idea per sentence.
- Specific nouns over adjectives.
- Present tense by default.
- Assume TTRPG literacy. Don't translate *initiative*, *party*, *roll*, *scene*, *DM*.
- Name the concrete. *The giant*, not "an encounter." *The tavern*, not "a social space."
- Acknowledge combat and narrative as two subjects the one voice covers. Neither gets softened or hyped when it's the subject.

### Forbidden moves

- No hype register: "powered by AI," "revolutionary," "next-generation," "ultimate," "epic."
- No exclamation points, except inside in-game dialogue.
- No gamer-speak, natural-20 humor, subreddit in-jokes.
- No first-person-plural "we" marketing. The team speaks in their own voices on social; the brand stays second-person.
- No softening combat into "tactical encounters" or "engagement modules." Combat is combat.
- No marketing the AI. The AI is infrastructure; the game is the product.
- No identity-presuming second-person ("as a busy professional…," "you're someone who…"). Second-person addresses the reader's *action* ("you roll," "you wake up"), not their demographic.

### Copy ownership

The voice architecture above is the design-system's responsibility. All actual brand copy — taglines, landing-page headers, product strings, feature announcements, marketing blurbs, error messages, welcome flows — is the brand owner's to write. This spec does not include sample copy.

## Tone calibration

If a brand decision feels too shiny, too loud, or too AI-coded, it's wrong for Mythrun. The brand frame is allowed to gesture at the game (pixel art, dithering, voxel-hinting) but never to shout. When in doubt, go quieter, smaller, more considered. The brand has permission to be a little strange, a little old-fashioned, a little craft-nerd. That's the register.
